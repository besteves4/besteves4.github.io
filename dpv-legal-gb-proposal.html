UK Data Protection Law extensions for DPV (UK GDPR, DPA 2018, PECR)

Abstract

This document proposes extending the Data Privacy Vocabulary [[DPV]] with the data protection law of the United Kingdom. It defines three law-specific vocabularies: gb-gdpr for the UK GDPR as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA), gb-dpa for a minimal slice of the Data Protection Act 2018, and gb-pecr for the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003. It also updates the existing legal-gb vocabulary. The design follows the EU GDPR extension [[EU-GDPR]].

Status of This Document

This is an unofficial proposal for discussion in the DPVCG. It is not a DPVCG deliverable. Concept names, counts and IRIs are provisional.

Legal accuracy. Legal statements come from the ICO [[ICO-DUAA]] and secondary summaries [[DUAA-DATED]] [[DUAA-COMMENCEMENT]]. They have not yet been checked line by line against legislation.gov.uk. Article sub-paragraph letters, the omission of Art. 77 UK GDPR, the PECR Schedule A1 exceptions and the Information Commission date need confirming before publication.

Introduction

DPV users cannot currently state a UK legal basis, right or transfer mechanism without borrowing eu-gdpr terms, which point at the wrong law. The current legal-gb vocabulary [[LEGAL-GB]] has only three concepts: law-DPA, law-GDPR and the authority DPA-GB.

Reusing eu-gdpr was a tolerable shortcut while the two texts were near-identical. Since the DUAA's main data protection provisions took effect on 5 February 2026 [[ICO-DUAA]], it gives wrong answers in several places:

  • Legal bases. UK Art. 6(1)(ea), recognised legitimate interests, has no EU counterpart and needs no balancing test.
  • Automated decisions. UK Arts. 22A to 22D replace the Art. 22 prohibition with a permission-plus-safeguards model.
  • Transfers. The UK uses adequacy regulations with a "not materially lower" data protection test, the IDTA and the UK Addendum.
  • Complaints. The right to complain moved to the DPA 2018, with a new right to complain to the controller first.
  • Governance. There is no one-stop-shop, lead authority or EDPB.

The extension serves organisations operating in both the EU and UK, UK health and research data users, and policy tooling (for example ODRL policies using DPV) that must name the applicable UK provision. It also addresses DPVCG issue 171 on consolidated legal bases and rights per jurisdiction.

Scope

The first release covers the UK GDPR as amended by the DUAA, the parts of the DPA 2018 that the UK GDPR cannot be used without, and the core PECR rules. The DUAA is in scope as a source of amendments, not as a vocabulary of its own.

LawRole in UK data protectionTreatmentPhase
UK GDPR [[UK-GDPR]]General processing regime: principles, legal bases, rights, transfersNew extension gb-gdpr1

Why the DPA 2018 is included. UK Art. 9(2)(b), (g), (h), (i) and (j) only apply when a DPA 2018 Schedule 1 condition is met. The exemptions from rights live in Schedules 2 to 4. After the DUAA, the right to complain to the controller is DPA 2018 s.164A, and the right to complain to the regulator sits in s.165 because the DUAA omits Art. 77 UK GDPR [[DUAA-COMMENCEMENT]].

Why the DUAA is not its own extension. Almost every data protection provision in the DUAA inserts or rewrites an article of the UK GDPR or a section of the DPA 2018. A reader citing "Art. 6(1)(ea) UK GDPR" expects to find it under the UK GDPR.

Commencement. Royal Assent was 19 June 2025. Most data protection provisions commenced on 5 February 2026, and the complaints procedure was due on 19 June 2026 [[ICO-DUAA]]. Secondary sources report the Information Commission replaced the ICO on 30 September 2026 [[DUAA-DATED]].

Design

The UK extension copies the two-layer pattern DPV uses for the EU [[LEGAL-EU]]: a jurisdiction vocabulary that lists laws and authorities, and law-specific vocabularies that hold the concepts of one law. UK concepts are defined independently of eu-gdpr and linked to it by SKOS mapping, never by subclassing, because the UK GDPR is a separate legal instrument.

Modelling rules

  1. IRIs follow the article number, as in eu-gdpr: gb-gdpr:A6-1-ea, gb-gdpr:A22A, gb-gdpr:A45A. DPA 2018 terms use section and schedule numbers: gb-dpa:S164A, gb-dpa:Sch1-P1-2.
  2. Parents are DPV core concepts. For example gb-gdpr:A6-1-a has parent dpv:Consent.
  3. Links to the EU GDPR use SKOS mapping properties: skos:exactMatch where the UK text is unchanged, skos:closeMatch where the DUAA altered it, and no mapping for UK-only concepts.
  4. Every concept carries its legal source via dct:source. Concepts added or changed by the DUAA also carry the DUAA as source and a dct:temporal start date equal to commencement.
  5. Replaced provisions are kept. The old gb-gdpr:A22 and gb-gdpr:A77 are published with an end date and dct:isReplacedBy, so earlier records remain valid.
  6. Labels say "UK GDPR" in full, to avoid confusion when both extensions are loaded.

Work plan

  1. Open a DPVCG GitHub issue with this proposal and link it to issue 171.
  2. Fix legal-gb (labels, dates, authority type, new laws).
  3. Draft the gb-gdpr concept spreadsheet by copying the eu-gdpr one, replacing labels and sources and marking each row exact, close or UK-only.
  4. Add the UK-only concepts: Art. 6(1)(ea) and Annex 1, Arts. 22A to 22D, transfers, the gb-dpa Schedule 1 and complaint concepts, and the gb-pecr concepts.
  5. Generate RDF and HTML with the DPV scripts, write the alignment file, and add the examples to the DPV examples set.
  6. Legal review of definitions against the consolidated legislation.gov.uk text, ideally by a UK practitioner, before the extension enters a DPV release.

Open issues for the DPVCG

  • Should DPV core gain a parent for "legitimate interest recognised by law", so gb-gdpr:A6-1-ea does not sit directly under dpv:LegalBasis? It is not dpv:LegitimateInterest, because no balancing test applies.
  • Keep gb-dpa separate, or fold the Schedule 1 conditions and complaint rights into gb-gdpr?
  • Should superseded provisions (pre-DUAA Art. 22 and Art. 77) be published as deprecated concepts with end dates, or left out?
  • Is the alignment with eu-gdpr published inside the extension or as a separate mapping file?
  • Should rights impacts be generated for the UK now or deferred?
  • How should the regulator be modelled across the ICO to Information Commission change: one concept relabelled, or two with dates?
